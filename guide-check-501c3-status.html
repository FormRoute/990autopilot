How to Check If Your Nonprofit Lost Its Tax-Exempt Status

A few minutes on the IRS site tells you whether your 501(c)(3) is still active — or was quietly revoked · Updated November 2026

If you've taken over a small nonprofit, inherited its books, or just realized no one has filed anything in a while, there's one question worth answering before anything else: is the organization still tax-exempt? The good news is you can check it yourself, for free, in a few minutes — no login, no accountant. Here's exactly how, and what each result means.

The fastest check: IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search

The IRS publishes a public database of every organization's exempt status. It's the same tool donors and grant-makers use to verify you.

  1. Go to the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (search "IRS TEOS" or visit apps.irs.gov/app/eos).
  2. Search by your organization's name or, more reliably, its EIN (the nine-digit number on your IRS determination letter).
  3. Read which database your organization shows up in — that's the part that actually tells you your status.

What each result means

The search labels organizations by the data set they appear in. Here's how to read it:

  • "Pub. 78 Data" or an active determination — you're recognized as tax-exempt and donations are deductible. You're in good standing.
  • "Form 990-N (e-Postcard)" filings listed — you can see the years your organization actually filed the e-Postcard. Gaps here are the warning sign: three consecutive missing years is what triggers revocation.
  • "Auto-Revocation List" — this is the one you don't want. It means the IRS automatically revoked your tax-exempt status for failing to file for three years in a row. The listing shows your revocation date and the date it was posted.
Important: appearing on the Auto-Revocation List does not always come with a letter in the mail. Many small nonprofits are revoked silently and only find out when a grant application bounces or a donor's accountant checks. Checking the list yourself is the only reliable way to know.

What to do based on what you find

You're active, with gaps or an approaching deadline

File the current year's 990-N now and get current. One or two missed years do not revoke your status — but never let it reach three. The simplest insurance is to stop relying on memory: have the filing handled automatically every year, so a board turnover or a quiet year can't cause a miss.

You're on the Auto-Revocation List

Common questions

Is there a cost to check my status?
No. The IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search and our deadline checker are both free. You only pay if you choose to have us handle the filing.
I can't find my organization at all. What does that mean?
Try searching by EIN rather than name, since names vary. If it truly doesn't appear, the organization may never have completed recognition, or the record may be under a slightly different legal name — worth confirming against your determination letter.
Will filing now fix a revoked status?
