Form 990-N vs 990-EZ: Which One Does Your Nonprofit File?

The size thresholds that decide which annual filing you owe — and why guessing wrong matters · Updated November 2026

Nearly every tax-exempt organization has to tell the IRS it still exists each year. Which form you use to do that depends almost entirely on one thing: how much money moves through your organization. Get the threshold right and the filing is trivial; get it wrong and you can either overwork a tiny all-volunteer nonprofit or, worse, file a notice the IRS doesn't consider sufficient. Here's the plain version.

The short answer

If your organization…You fileWhat it is
Gross receipts normally $50,000 or lessForm 990-N (e-Postcard)A short electronic notice — about eight pieces of basic information.
Gross receipts under $200,000 and total assets under $500,000Form 990-EZA multi-page financial return (revenue, expenses, balance sheet, program detail).
Gross receipts $200,000+ or assets $500,000+Form 990 (full)The complete annual information return.

Private foundations are a separate case — they file Form 990-PF regardless of size. Churches and certain affiliated organizations may not be required to file at all. When in doubt about those edge cases, check with a CPA.

What "normally $50,000 or less" actually means

This trips people up. The IRS doesn't look at a single year in isolation — it applies an averaging test. In broad terms, if your organization's average annual gross receipts over the relevant period are $50,000 or less, you're a 990-N filer. So one unusually good year — a big one-time grant, say — doesn't automatically bump you up to the 990-EZ. It's the running average that counts.

Gross receipts, not net. This is the total money your organization took in before any expenses — donations, grants, program fees, everything. A group that raised $48,000 and spent $47,000 still measures at $48,000 in gross receipts, comfortably in 990-N territory.

Why it's worth getting right

  • Filing too high wastes effort. A tiny volunteer-run nonprofit that mistakenly thinks it must file the 990-EZ takes on a real financial return it doesn't owe — hours of bookkeeping it could skip.
  • Filing too low can be insufficient. If you've genuinely grown past the $50,000 average and keep filing only the 990-N, the IRS may not treat your annual obligation as met — and unmet annual filings are what lead to automatic revocation after three years.
  • The deadline is the same either way: the 15th day of the 5th month after your fiscal year ends. (See 990-N due dates by fiscal year.)

What each form actually asks for

Form 990-N (e-Postcard)

Eight items, filed electronically: your EIN, legal name and any other names used, mailing address, the name and address of a principal officer, your website (if any), the tax year, and a confirmation that your gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less (plus a note if the organization is going out of business). No financial statements, no attachments. It takes minutes.

Form 990-EZ

A genuine return: a revenue and expense statement, a balance sheet, a summary of program-service accomplishments, and information about officers, directors, and key people — often with additional schedules. Most organizations at this level use bookkeeping software or a preparer.

Common questions

We're brand new with almost no income — do we still file?
Yes. Once the IRS recognizes your organization, you have an annual filing obligation even with $0 in receipts. For most new small nonprofits that's the 990-N, and filing it keeps your status alive while you grow.
Can I choose to file the 990-EZ voluntarily?
You can file a 990-EZ (or full 990) even if you qualify for the 990-N — some organizations do for transparency with funders. You just can't go the other direction once your average receipts exceed the $50,000 line.
