Does Your Booster Club or PTA Have to File Form 990-N?

Short answer: almost certainly yes — and these groups are the ones that lose their status most often · Updated November 2026

Booster clubs and PTAs are classic small nonprofits: a volunteer board, a modest bank account, and a treasurer who rotates every year or two. That setup is exactly why so many of them quietly lose their tax-exempt status — not because they did anything wrong, but because one annual filing slipped through a handoff. Here's whether the rule applies to you, the one real exception, and how to make sure it never becomes a problem.

If you have your own EIN, you almost certainly file

The test is simple. If your booster club or PTA is recognized as a 501(c)(3), has its own EIN (Employer Identification Number), and normally takes in $50,000 or less a year, it must file the Form 990-N (e-Postcard) with the IRS every single year — even in a quiet year with a small fundraiser and nothing else.

A few things that don't change this: being "attached" to a school, running everything through parent volunteers, having no paid staff, or having a tiny budget. If the club is its own legal entity with its own EIN, it has its own filing obligation, separate from the school or district.

Why these groups get revoked so often: the treasurer who knew about the 990-N graduates out with their kid, the new treasurer never hears about it, and three years later the club discovers — usually when trying to open a new bank account or apply for a grant — that the IRS revoked its status.

The one real exception: a group exemption

Some local units don't file on their own because they're covered by a group exemption held by a parent organization. This is most common with PTAs: many local PTAs fall under their state or national PTA's group ruling, and the parent either files a group return or directs how each unit reports. National service and youth organizations sometimes work the same way.

This is worth confirming rather than assuming. If you're a PTA, ask your state PTA office whether your unit is covered by the group exemption and who is responsible for the annual filing. If you're an independent booster club that was formed on its own and got its own determination letter, you are almost certainly filing for yourself.

Common questions

We only raised a few hundred dollars this year. Still file?
Yes. The 990-N is required as long as you're a recognized tax-exempt organization with your own EIN, even in a year with almost no activity. A quiet year is simply a 990-N that confirms the quiet.
