Questions

Is my organization eligible for Form 990-N?

Most tax-exempt organizations whose annual gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less can file Form 990-N instead of the longer Form 990 or 990-EZ. "Normally" is an IRS 3-year average test. Churches and certain other organizations are not required to file at all. If you're unsure, check your determination letter or ask a tax professional — we can't make this determination for you.

What if we grow past $50,000?

Then you'll need Form 990-EZ or 990 instead, which we don't file. Our annual "anything changed?" email asks about this specifically — if you've grown, we pause your subscription (no charge for that year) and point you to appropriate options. That's a good problem to have.

We already missed our deadline. Is it too late?

No — and this surprises people: there's no monetary penalty for filing a 990-N late. What matters is not missing three consecutive years, which triggers automatic revocation of tax-exempt status. File now and the year counts.

We haven't filed for two years. Can you help?

Yes — file the current year now, which stops the revocation clock. Note the IRS system only accepts the current and most recent prior year for 990-N; older years can't be filed retroactively, but that's okay: what matters going forward is an unbroken record, which is exactly what Autopilot maintains.

Can't we just file free on IRS.gov ourselves?

Yes, you can — the IRS offers free direct filing, and we tell you that plainly. What you're paying us $29 for is never having to remember it: the monitoring, the reminders, the automatic filing when officers change and institutional memory doesn't carry over. Organizations lose their exempt status not because filing is hard, but because remembering is.
