Do Nonprofit Chapters & Affiliates File Their Own 990-N?

If your club belongs to a bigger national organization, here's who actually files — and the assumption that quietly revokes chapters · Updated November 2026

Lions clubs, Rotary and Kiwanis clubs, PTAs, booster clubs, scout units, fraternity and sorority chapters, 4-H groups — they all share a structure: a small local unit that belongs to a much larger national body. And they all share a dangerous assumption: that because there's a national office, someone up there is handling the IRS filing. Usually, no one is. Here's how it actually works.

The rule: your EIN is what decides

A local chapter that has its own EIN and is recognized as tax-exempt has its own annual filing obligation — normally the Form 990-N if it takes in $50,000 or less a year. The national organization's existence does not remove that obligation. The only thing that changes it is whether you're covered by a group exemption and included in a group return.

What a group exemption is (and isn't)

A group exemption is a single IRS ruling that covers a central organization and its subordinate chapters together, under a shared Group Exemption Number (GEN). It's how a national body can extend tax-exempt status to hundreds of local units without each one filing its own application.

But a group exemption for your status is not the same as the parent filing your annual return. There are two scenarios:

  • The central organization files a group return that includes your chapter — then you don't file your own 990-N for those years.
  • The central organization does not include you in a group return — then, even though you're under its group exemption, your chapter files its own 990-N under its own EIN.

The second case is extremely common, and it's where chapters get caught: they're "under" the national group exemption, so they assume filing is covered — but no group return is actually being filed for them.

The assumption that revokes chapters: "We're part of [national organization], so they handle the IRS stuff." Three quiet years later — usually after a couple of treasurer handoffs — the chapter is on the IRS Auto-Revocation List, and the national office confirms it never filed on the chapter's behalf.

How to tell which applies to you

  1. Ask your national or state office directly: "Are we included in a group return, or do we file our own 990-N?" Get it in writing.
  2. Check the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search by your chapter's EIN to see your own filing history and whether you appear on the Auto-Revocation List. Here's how to check your status.
  3. When in doubt, file. Filing a 990-N you technically didn't need to is harmless; missing one you did need is what causes revocation.

Common questions

Our national office has a group exemption — doesn't that cover our filing?
It covers your tax-exempt status, not necessarily your annual return. Unless the central organization files a group return that includes you, your chapter files its own 990-N. Confirm with them in writing.
We have our own EIN but never filed anything. Are we at risk?
