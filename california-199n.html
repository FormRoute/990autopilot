The California filing most nonprofits don't know they still owe

For small California nonprofits that already file the federal 990-N · Updated October 2026

If your organization is based in California, filing the federal Form 990-N with the IRS only takes care of half of your annual obligation. California wants its own short annual return — FTB Form 199N, the state's own "California e-Postcard" — and it's filed separately, with the state, every year.

It's easy to miss precisely because it looks like the federal one you may have already handled. Many boards file with the IRS, assume they're done, and never realize California was expecting a filing too.

The stakes are the same as the federal filing. Miss the California 199N three years in a row and the Franchise Tax Board can revoke your California tax-exempt status — which can mean owing the state's minimum franchise tax and going through reinstatement. Losing federal status and losing state status are two separate risks, and the 199N is what protects the state side.

Common questions

Isn't the federal 990-N enough?
No. The federal 990-N satisfies the IRS. California is a separate government with its own annual filing (FTB 199N). Both are required for a California nonprofit.
Do I need the California add-on if I'm not in California?
No — this applies only to organizations formed or operating in California. Other states have their own rules, which we're happy to look into as we add them.
Is there a separate charity registration too?
Many California charities also renew annually with the Attorney General's Registry of Charitable Trusts (Form RRF-1), which is separate from the 199N. Ask us if you'd like help understanding what applies to you.
What do you need from me?
Your EIN and California corporation/registration number, plus the same basic details on your federal filing. We confirm everything before anything is submitted.
